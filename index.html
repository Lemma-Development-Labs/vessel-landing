04 — WHY VESSEL EXISTS
“If you can't verify the hedge, you don't own the yield.”
— THE VESSEL THESIS

Every synthetic dollar asks for trust in the same three places: an admin key that can mint unbacked supply, collateral you can't independently price, and a hedge book parked at a custodian you will never see inside.

When those fail, they fail silently. You learn about the hole from a blog post, weeks after withdrawals froze. An attestation is a photograph of a balance sheet, taken by the people who own it.

Vessel's answer is architectural, not procedural. No privileged mint. Collateral on-chain. The entire short book on a public order book, where anyone can re-derive the hedge, block by block.

05 — FAQ

Honest answers

What happens when funding goes negative?+

Ballast eats it first, by design — that is what its levered upside pays for. If negative funding exhausts Ballast, the protocol reserve absorbs the next tranche. Hull is last in line and keeps its fixed rate until those layers are gone.

Can Vessel mint vUSD out of thin air?+

No. There is no privileged mint function and no admin key that can expand supply. Every unit is minted against collateral that is already on-chain and already hedged.

How do I check the hedge myself?+

Read Vessel's vault addresses in the explorer, then read the matching short positions on the perp order book. Both legs are public state — you can re-derive net delta yourself at any block height, without asking us for anything.

What are the real risks?+

Sustained negative funding, perp-venue liquidity and liquidation risk, spot-perp basis dislocation, oracle failure, and smart-contract risk. Vessel removes custodial and mint-authority risk; it does not remove market risk. The litepaper states each one plainly.

Why Monad?+

Because continuous, verifiable rehedging is only economic where blocks are fast and gas is near-zero. 400ms blocks let the engine hold delta tight and write every adjustment to public state instead of batching it out of sight.
