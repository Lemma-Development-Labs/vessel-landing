1Abstract

Vessel is a delta-neutral yield protocol native to Monad. It holds spot assets and shorts the equivalent notional on Perpl — Monad's fully on-chain central limit order book for perpetual futures — capturing the funding that leveraged longs pay, plus staking yield when the spot leg is a liquid staking token. The position carries near-zero price exposure; its return is the market price of leverage.

Vessel structures this yield stream into two tranches. Hull is senior: a fixed rate to maturity, protected by subordination — losses reach it last. Ballast is junior: it absorbs negative funding and drawdowns first and earns levered residual yield for doing so. Hull is protection sold as a fixed-income product rather than as insurance; Ballast is underwriting sold as levered yield. Nobody pays a premium; nobody files a claim.

Vessel's defining property is verifiability. Existing synthetic-dollar designs hedge on centralized venues behind custodians; solvency is an attestation. Vessel's entire short book lives on a fully on-chain order book, so the hedge — every position, every funding payment — is independently checkable, block by block. In phase three, Vessel mints vUSD, a dollar backed by this verifiable book.

2Background

2.1 The funding rate is DeFi's most persistent real yield. Perpetual futures balance longs and shorts through funding: when leverage demand skews long — the structural norm in crypto — longs pay shorts a periodic rate. Delta-neutral capture of this rate is the engine behind the largest synthetic-dollar protocol (Ethena, multi-billion TVL at peak), and behind Kimia's model on Solana. The yield is real: it is paid by identifiable counterparties who want leverage.

2.2 The trust problem. Incumbent basis engines hedge on centralized exchanges via off-exchange settlement providers. Users receive custodial attestations, not proofs. The failure mode of opacity is well documented across CeDeFi: privileged admin keys, unverifiable collateral, hedge books nobody can inspect. A yield product's core claim — "we are hedged" — should be checkable, not certified.

2.3 Why this becomes possible on Monad now. Monad (mainnet Nov 24, 2025) runs a parallel EVM at 10,000 TPS with 400ms blocks and sub-second finality, at near-zero fees [source: docs.monad.xyz; live network data Aug 2026]. Perpl uses this to run matching and settlement fully on-chain, with market-maker post+cancel under 100k gas [source: docs.perpl.xyz]. For the first time on an EVM L1, the hedge venue itself is transparent — and cheap enough to rebalance continuously. Pendle deployed on Monad in 2026 with initial dollar pools, meaning a fixed-rate distribution layer already exists and needs native yield feedstock. Monad's TVL approached $1B in Aug 2026 with no native funding-capture engine [GATE-0: competitor check — confirm no live native basis vault].

3Protocol design

3.1 The engine

For each supported market m (launch set: ETH; expansion: BTC, MON [GATE-0: confirm Perpl testnet/mainnet market list]):

  1. Spot leg. Buy the asset (or its LST — shMON/gMON for MON [GATE-0: LST contract addresses + liquidity]) via Kuru/Uniswap on Monad.
  2. Short leg. Short equal notional on Perpl with USDC margin at leverage λ.
  3. Neutrality. Maintain |net delta| ≤ 1% of gross notional; rebalance on band breach or a 4-hour timer, whichever first.

Capital construction. Per $1 of deployed TVL with short-leg leverage λ:

  • spot fraction = λ/(1+λ), short margin = 1/(1+λ), hedged notional = λ/(1+λ).
  • Launch policy: λ ≤ 2 (spot 66.7% / margin 33.3% / hedged notional 66.7% per $1).
  • A 10% idle USDC buffer is held unhedged for withdrawals and margin top-ups; deployed ratio ≤ 90%.

Gross yield per period: G = hedged_notional × (funding_rate + staking_rate_if_LST) − trading_costs − keeper_costs Funding can be negative; the structure in §3.2 exists because of that.

Liquidation policy. Margin is maintained such that the short's liquidation price sits ≥ 40% above mark [GATE-0: derive exact maintenance-margin math from Perpl's liquidation engine docs]. The keeper tops up margin from the idle buffer before the threshold; a breach of the buffer triggers automatic de-risking (partial unwind), never added leverage.

3.2 Tranches: Hull and Ballast

Deposits enter a USDC-denominated ERC-4626 vault and are allocated to one of two claims on the engine:

Hull (senior).

  • Issued in fixed-maturity series (launch: 4-week; later 12-week) as transferable series tokens.
  • Pays a fixed rate rH set at issuance. Launch policy: r_H = clamp(EWMA_30d(net funding APR) × (1 − h), 0, r_cap) with haircut h = 40% and cap r_cap = 15% APR [GATE-0: calibrate from real Perpl funding history]. Later series move to a Dutch auction so the market sets rH.
  • Redeems principal + fixed yield at maturity. Early exit only via secondary sale (Pendle-compatible by design).

Ballast (junior).

  • Perpetual rolling tranche; receives everything above Hull's promise; absorbs everything below it, first.
  • Withdrawals subject to a 48h cooldown and to the subordination floor (below).

The waterfall, each accrual epoch (daily accrual, continuous NAV):

G (gross yield)
 ├─ 1. Protocol fee: 10% of max(G, 0)  → half to Reserve until target, rest to treasury
 ├─ 2. Hull accrual: A_H = r_H × H × Δt
 └─ 3. Residual (G' − A_H) → Ballast   [levered upside]

If G' < A_H (shortfall S):
 1. Ballast NAV absorbs S
 2. then Reserve absorbs S
 3. only then Hull NAV is impaired (a formally-defined credit event, disclosed on-chain)

Subordination constraint. B / (H + B) ≥ θ_min = 20% at all times. New Hull issuance is capped by available Ballast; Ballast withdrawals that would breach θmin queue until capacity exists. θmin is the attachment point that makes Hull's protection quantifiable: Hull is impaired only after Ballast (≥20% of TVL) and the Reserve are exhausted.

Reserve. Target = 2% of TVL, funded from the protocol-fee split; exists to smooth transient negative-funding epochs so Ballast NAV isn't whipsawed by noise.

Conservation invariant (enforced in code and fuzzed): ΔNAV_Hull + ΔNAV_Ballast + ΔReserve + fees = G for every epoch, no exceptions.

3.3 Why Hull is cover, not insurance

On-chain insurance failed on three axes: nobody wants to pay premiums, claims processes rot, and underwriting capital never shows up. Hull dissolves all three. The "premium" is the spread between expected funding and rH — paid invisibly, inside a product people already want (a fixed rate). The "claim" is the waterfall — automatic, rule-based, no adjudication. The "underwriter" is Ballast — capital that shows up because first-loss is compensated with levered yield, not with gratitude. Cover as capital structure.

3.4 vUSD (phase 3, post-audit)

vUSD is a dollar minted 1:1 against the delta-neutral book, over-collateralized by the Ballast + Reserve cushion, redeemable via engine unwind. Design constraints, fixed now: no privileged mint path exists in any Vessel contract — vUSD supply changes only through the collateralized mint/redeem flow; parameters sit behind a timelock; the only guardian power is pause (which cannot move funds). A staked variant (svUSD) directs the engine yield to stakers. vUSD's endgame utilities: Pendle pools (distribution) and Perpl margin collateral (flywheel) — both subject to those teams' listings, stated as goals, not facts.

5Risk (stated plainly)

  1. Funding inversion. Extended negative funding drains Ballast, then Reserve. Mitigations: multi-market diversification, per-market caps, de-risk rules, and the honest one — Hull's rate haircut exists because funding is volatile. The Nov 2026 MON unlock may invert MON funding specifically; the MON market launches capped and last.
  2. Liquidation/gap risk on the short leg. Mitigated by λ ≤ 2, the 40% liquidation buffer, 400ms rebalance granularity — not eliminated.
  3. Venue risk. Vessel's hedge is verifiable, but it lives on Perpl's contracts; a Perpl failure is a Vessel failure. We do not pretend otherwise — the transparency claim is about observability, not invincibility.
  4. Basis risk. Spot leg and perp mark can diverge; LST legs add depeg risk.
  5. Vessel contract risk. Unaudited until the external review that gates mainnet. Testnet only until then.
  6. Liquidity risk. Hull is illiquid to maturity by design; Ballast exits are cooldown- and subordination-constrained by design.
  7. Regulatory. vUSD (phase 3) is a synthetic dollar; jurisdictional analysis precedes its mainnet, not its whitepaper section.

6Fees and business model

  • Protocol fee: 10% of gross positive yield (Reserve takes half until target).
  • Hull spread: the gap between realized net funding and rH accrues to the waterfall (Ballast + protocol), which is why conservative rH setting is also the revenue engine.
  • Execution rebate: Vessel attaches its own Perpl builder code to all engine flow (builder-fee fields are native in Perpl's API), recapturing basis points on its own execution.
  • Phase 3: vUSD mint/redeem fee (single-digit bps) and svUSD fee share.
  • B2B ("fix this rate"): an embeddable module letting any Monad frontend offer Hull at deposit time for a revenue share — the distribution model Monad's own research proposed for on-chain protection, applied to yield.

Revenue = TVL × net yield × 10% + Hull spread capture + rebates. No projected figures appear here until Gate-0 supplies real funding data; the illustrative model lives in the thesis document, clearly labeled.

7Roadmap

  • Phase 0 — Gates (now). Perpl OI + funding history via API; competitor scan; oracle census; liquidation math. Any failed gate is a published STOP, not a workaround.
  • Phase 1 — Testnet MVP (weeks 1–6). Single ETH market, Hull series-001 (4-week), Ballast, waterfall, proof-of-hedge dashboard. Spec: see MVP document.
  • Phase 2 — Hardened testnet → external review. Invariant/fuzz suite, negative-funding simulations, liquidation drills; review is a hard mainnet gate.
  • Phase 3 — Capped mainnet. Deposit caps, θmin raised, Pendle listing conversation, grant applications with live metrics.
  • Phase 4 — vUSD + multi-market + auctions.

8Glossary

Funding — periodic payment between perp longs and shorts. Delta-neutral — price exposure ≈ 0. Hull — senior fixed-rate tranche. Ballast — junior first-loss tranche. θmin — minimum Ballast share of TVL (subordination floor). rH — Hull's fixed rate. vUSD — the dollar minted against the hedged book. Proof-of-Hedge — the per-block public reconstruction of Vessel's positions.
